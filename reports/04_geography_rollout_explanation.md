# Module 04 — Geography Rollout Testing

## Objective

This module simulates data-driven testing over the rollout of corporate AML lifecycle standards across geographies.

The objective is to identify countries, regions or business areas where AML lifecycle standards have not been implemented, have been delayed, lack local ownership or present unresolved remediation gaps.

---

## Why This Matters

AML Holding functions do not only define policies and standards.

They must also ensure that corporate AML standards are communicated, deployed, understood and implemented across different geographies.

A standard that exists at Holding level but is not implemented locally creates a governance gap.

This is especially relevant in global financial institutions, where AML/CFT risk must be managed consistently across jurisdictions, business lines and customer segments.

---

## Tests Performed

### 1. Geographies where AML lifecycle standard has not been implemented

Identifies countries or regions where the corporate AML lifecycle standard has not yet been implemented.

This may indicate rollout delays, local resistance, operational constraints or lack of governance follow-up.

---

### 2. Rollout implementation deadline missed

Identifies geographies where the implementation deadline has passed but the rollout is not complete.

This may indicate weak project tracking, insufficient escalation or lack of delivery discipline.

---

### 3. Corporate standard communicated but not implemented

Identifies cases where the corporate AML standard has been communicated but implementation has not been completed.

This may indicate a gap between communication and actual adoption.

---

### 4. Geographies without local owner assigned

Identifies geographies where no local owner has been assigned for the rollout.

This may indicate weak accountability and unclear responsibility for implementation.

---

### 5. Geographies with open rollout gaps and overdue remediation

Identifies countries or regions with open implementation gaps where remediation deadlines have already passed.

This may indicate unresolved control weaknesses or poor remediation management.

---

### 6. Implemented geographies with unresolved post-implementation gaps

Identifies geographies marked as implemented but still showing open gaps.

This may indicate that implementation status may be overstated or that post-implementation issues have not been fully remediated.

---

## Governance Interpretation

Rollout governance is a critical part of AML Lifecycle Transformation.

At Holding level, the challenge is not only to design a corporate standard, but to ensure that the standard is translated into local execution.

Effective rollout requires:

* clear ownership;
* communication;
* implementation deadlines;
* tracking of open gaps;
* remediation plans;
* escalation of delays;
* post-implementation review.

Without these elements, AML standards may exist on paper but fail in practice.

---

## Potential Findings

Potential governance findings may include:

* AML lifecycle standards not implemented in certain geographies;
* missed implementation deadlines;
* lack of local ownership;
* communication performed but no actual implementation;
* unresolved rollout gaps;
* overdue remediation actions;
* geographies marked as implemented despite open gaps.

---

## Recommended Management Actions

Recommended actions may include:

1. Define clear rollout milestones for each geography.
2. Assign local owners for every AML lifecycle standard.
3. Track implementation status through governance reporting.
4. Escalate overdue implementation deadlines.
5. Monitor open gaps and remediation actions.
6. Validate post-implementation effectiveness.
7. Ensure local adoption is evidenced, not only communicated.

---

## Interview Explanation

I included this module because the AML Lifecycle Transformation role refers to the deployment of corporate AML standards across geographies.

The purpose of this module is to show how SQL can be used to test whether AML lifecycle standards are being implemented consistently across countries, with clear ownership, deadlines, remediation tracking and governance follow-up.

The objective is not to assess a real BBVA rollout, but to demonstrate how data analytics can support AML Holding by identifying implementation gaps, overdue remediation actions and weaknesses in rollout governance.
